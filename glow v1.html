Safety

How loud, and where to put it

Research on infant sound machines found every device tested went above the level recommended for hospital nurseries at maximum volume, 30 cm away. Placement matters more than anything on the box.

  • Across the room, never in the cot.
  • Around 50 dB or below for all-night use near a baby — about a quiet fridge. If you can talk over it, it's loud enough.
  • Turn on the child lock so the volume can't be changed.

Before you decide

Questions, answered plainly

White, pink or brown noise — what's the difference?

All three are steady, broadband sound; they differ in where the energy sits. White is even across the range and covers sharp sounds best. Pink leans lower and sounds softer, which suits traffic. Brown is lower again, closest to a distant waterfall. You can hear all three above.

Can it run all night? Can sound and light work separately?

Yes to both. Leave it running continuously or set a 30, 60 or 90-minute timer, and use sound on its own, light on its own, or together.

Do I need the app?

No — every function is on the touch panel. The app adds schedules, okay-to-wake programs, child lock and control from another room, and needs Wi-Fi to reach the machine. Share one machine between phones, or run up to five machines from one phone. Works with Alexa and Google Home.

Battery life, and charging?

Up to 10 hours on a charge over USB-C, and it keeps playing while plugged in. If the power goes out it switches to battery on its own.

How loud should I set it for a baby?

Around 50 dB or below for all-night use, placed across the room rather than near the cot. More in the safety section above.
